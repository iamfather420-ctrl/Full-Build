# NIST AI RMF terminology (AI glossary): intake assessment

**Checked:** 2026-11-25. **Decision: HUMAN_LICENSE_REVIEW; do not import the spreadsheet yet.** The official downloadable component is NIST’s *The Language of Trustworthy AI: An In-Depth Glossary of Terms*, a separate beta spreadsheet associated with the AI RMF. NIST states that the beta is still being developed and that a final glossary release will come later. Its live published sheet reports “updated August 4, 2024”; no stable final version or immutable dated spreadsheet snapshot is identified on the official glossary page. [1] [2] [3]

## Version, scope, and acquisition

For the AI RMF itself, the latest released framework identified on NIST’s current page remains **AI RMF 1.0**, released January 26, 2023. The same page says the framework is being revised, but identifies no released successor; the PDF describes it as a living document and specifies major/minor version numbering. Thus AI RMF 1.0 is the latest stable framework version, while the specific terminology spreadsheet remains beta rather than a stable/versioned dataset. [1] [4]

The official glossary page links to a direct Excel export: [XLSX](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/e/2PACX-1vTRBYglcOtgaMrdF11aFxfEY3EmB31zslYI4q2_7ZZ8z_1lKm7OHtF0t4xIsckuogNZ3hRZAaDQuv_K/pub?output=xlsx). It also offers CSV and PDF. These are live Google Sheets publication endpoints, not immutable snapshots. A headers-only check on the XLSX endpoint on 2026-11-25 returned no usable content length, so no byte size is asserted; the artifact is a small spreadsheet and is technically feasible to store. NIST’s March 2023 development report records **511 terms with at least one definition, 218 with at least two, and 379 bibliography citations** at that report’s publication. It describes multiple definition, legal-definition, synonym, related-term, note, and citation fields. Those counts describe the 2023 report, not a verified current row count. [2] [3] [5]

## Rights and suitability

No dataset-specific license, Creative Commons grant, or explicit spreadsheet redistribution/modification permission was found on the official glossary page or in the directly linked publication documentation. NIST’s general website notice says unmarked information on NIST sites is public information that may be distributed or copied, with credits requested. But NIST’s research-product policy is more qualified: NIST-employee-authored works are generally not copyrighted in the United States, while some NIST publications are written by third parties and may be subject to copyright; foreign rights are reserved, with a stated public license for NIST employee works to the extent NIST asserts foreign rights. The glossary report credits both NIST staff and BNH.AI authors. [6] [7]

Crucially, the development report says definitions were drawn from NIST, standards bodies including IEEE/ISO/ANSI, peer-reviewed works, textbooks, and other sources, with citations recorded in the workbook. It does not establish a blanket license for each copied or adapted definition or for the complete spreadsheet. Accordingly, **commercial use, redistribution, and modification of the spreadsheet contents are unclear or conditional**, not affirmatively cleared. Public availability, a government host, and free download do not by themselves resolve rights in each component. This is a rights-screening assessment, not legal advice. [2] [5] [6]

**Recommendation:** retain metadata only until a human reviewer determines rights for the exact spreadsheet and its cited definitions, including third-party content and the spreadsheet’s co-authored/contracted contributions. If cleared, preserve the source title, NIST AI 100-3 report/DOI, glossary source URL and export URL, access date, reported sheet update date, all term-level citation tags and the bibliography tab, and an integrity hash for the acquired snapshot. Do not flatten away provenance or present the multi-definition glossary as a single authoritative definition per term. Import feasibility is high; rights clearance and beta/version reproducibility are the blockers. No dataset was downloaded, no acquisition script was run, no repository was changed, and no training was performed.
