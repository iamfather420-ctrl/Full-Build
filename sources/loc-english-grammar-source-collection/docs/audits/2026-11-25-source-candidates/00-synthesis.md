# Language-resource candidate audit — synthesis

**Assessment date reflected in the source assessments:** 2026-11-25  
**Scope:** Synthesis of the returned assessments only. No additional research was performed, no rights gaps are inferred away, and this is an assessment of candidate resources—not a license guarantee or legal advice.

## Executive decision

Four bounded subsets are assessed as suitable for acquisition subject to their stated scope and provenance conditions: Wikidata Lexemes (CC0), the LLM Technical Glossary Markdown subset (CC0), three O*NET 31.0 tables (CC BY 4.0), and the GSA Plain Language Guidelines Markdown subset (CC0). The remaining candidates are **blocked from data import or redistribution pending human license review**; OpenStax *Principles of Management* is metadata-only under its current terms, and the other conditional candidates should likewise remain metadata-only until cleared. Public availability, a permissive label in a secondary catalog, or a broad institutional policy is not by itself sufficient to resolve the open rights questions described below.

The recommended acquisition order is: (1) O*NET 31.0 selected tables; (2) GSA Plain Language Guidelines Markdown; (3) LLM Technical Glossary Markdown; (4) Wikidata Lexemes snapshot. This order favors small, fixed, directly scoped subsets before the larger lexical snapshot. It is a recommendation for later, authorized acquisition—not a statement that any files have been acquired. Preserve all source IDs and provenance fields, and keep out-of-scope code, media, and separately licensed components excluded.

**Existing state:** The original six Library of Congress (LOC) records are to remain unchanged. Full-Build remains untouched while its Daisy training run is active. The supplied assessments report no acquisition or training-script runs and no repository changes. The failure list is empty.

## Recommended acquisition order

1. **O*NET 31.0 named tables.** Best first bounded acquisition by compactness, structured identifiers, current fixed release, and expressly scoped CC BY 4.0 terms. Keep to the three listed tables and retain attribution, source codes, checksums, and O*NET-SOC identifiers. This does not extend the license conclusion to other O*NET content.
2. **GSA Plain Language Guidelines repository Markdown.** Compact and expressly CC0 under the pinned GSA repository license. Keep the PDF, images, and media excluded because their rights were not independently verified; treat the 2011 edition identification cautiously.
3. **LLM Technical Glossary Markdown.** Small, relevant specialist supplement with explicit CC0 and a commit-pinned source. Retain entry-level links and review for third-party verbatim material; unsigned commit and possible content provenance remain caveats.
4. **Wikidata Lexemes snapshot.** Explicit CC0 for the relevant structured-data namespace and a dated, reproducible snapshot, but larger (~495 MB compressed), uneven in coverage/quality, and subject to unresolved upstream-rights claims. Keep the dump to the Lexemes component and preserve IDs, references, date, and checksum.

**Do not place blocked candidates in the acquisition queue until cleared.** In particular, the UD English-EWT text-bearing rows, WordNet 3.1 license attachment, CMUdict license-document scope, FrameNet Berkeley/NLTK discrepancy, NIST glossary third-party definitions, SBA page boundaries, NASA mixed-authorship rights, and NIST SSDF contributor/territorial rights each require a human decision. OpenStax management, accounting, and statistics additionally require written permission for the stated LLM ingestion use; the management record is metadata-only under its current assessment.

## Failures and material open questions

- **Failures:** The supplied assessment set reports `failures: []`. No candidate data were reported acquired; no acquisition or training scripts were run. This synthesis did not verify source endpoints or perform downloads.
- **WordNet:** Does the Release 3.0 license text govern the 3.1 database-only archive, and what exact notice/attribution terms apply to that archive?
- **UD English-EWT:** What rights cover the underlying source texts embedded in CoNLL-U rows, including commercial redistribution and modification? CC BY-SA annotations/database rights do not answer that question.
- **CMUdict:** How do the README’s data-specific permission and repository LICENSE map to the exact `cmudict.dict` data, and which obligations control? GitHub’s `NOASSERTION` status does not resolve it.
- **FrameNet:** Will Berkeley confirm in writing the applicable license, commercial redistribution/modification rights, and component scope for the exact v1.7 package despite the NLTK catalog’s CC BY 3.0 label?
- **NIST AI RMF glossary:** What rights apply to the exact beta spreadsheet and to each third-party-sourced definition? Is a stable final snapshot available, and what are its actual current size/counts?
- **OpenStax books:** Written permission is needed for commercial and LLM ingestion uses identified in the title notices. If granted, which art, activities, figures, and other components have separate rights, and which PDF/web revision is authorized?
- **SBA:** Which exact live-page text is verified SBA-authored government information, and which embedded/linked material is contributed or licensed by third parties? The policy alone is not an item-level rights manifest.
- **NASA Systems Engineering Handbook:** What permissions apply to contractor-authored or other third-party contributions, and how should NASA STI/source acknowledgment and newer SI attribution guidance be applied to the intended corpus/output?
- **NIST SSDF:** Does the publication’s U.S. public-domain statement and NIST foreign-rights language cover the exact work and intended commercial/AI use, and are any rights held by the non-NIST author or third parties? Supplemental files require separate review.
- **Subset candidates:** O*NET title-source provenance, potential upstream claims in Wikidata and CC0 glossary material, and the precise edition/provenance of the GSA Markdown subset remain stated caveats. These do not negate the assessment’s explicit license decision for the named subsets, but any concrete third-party claim or separately licensed content should be escalated rather than assumed clear.
