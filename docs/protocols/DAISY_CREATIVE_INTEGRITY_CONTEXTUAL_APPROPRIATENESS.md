# Daisy Creative Integrity & Contextual Appropriateness Protocol (CICAP)

**Protocol ID:** SOLVEX-DAISY-CICAP  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Implemented as a deterministic policy contract; evaluator integrations remain a separate requirement.

## Purpose

Permit useful imagination without allowing creativity to become a source of fabricated facts, irrelevant output, inappropriate responses, false software results, or unauthorized actions. Creativity is a capability, not an exemption from task, truth, safety, or authorization requirements.

## Six creativity modes

| Mode | Permitted purpose | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| GENERATIVE | Create ideas, designs, drafts, and novel approaches | Label new material as proposed unless independently verified |
| EXPLORATORY | Explore alternatives and ask “what if?” | Keep hypotheses distinct from established facts |
| ANALYTICAL | Interpret supplied or retrieved information | Do not add unsupported facts |
| SYNTHETIC | Combine evidence and ideas into a new solution | Preserve provenance and distinguish inference from source facts |
| SIMULATIVE | Model scenarios and hypothetical outcomes | Mark results SIMULATED; never represent them as real execution |
| EXECUTABLE | Prepare or perform authorized software/system work | Require explicit authorization and record actual execution evidence |

## Required output labels

- **VERIFIED** — supported by identifiable evidence and a passing evidence-integrity assessment.
- **PROPOSED** — a newly designed option that has not been established as fact.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — a plausible explanation or prediction requiring investigation.
- **SIMULATED** — generated by a model, mock, sandbox scenario, or hypothetical calculation rather than the claimed real-world operation.
- **UNVERIFIED** — insufficient evidence to establish correctness.

A label must not be upgraded because an answer sounds confident or internally consistent.

## Mandatory contextual review

Before output is allowed, the policy boundary requires positive assessments of:

1. **Task relevance:** Does the content answer the actual request and stay within its scope?
2. **Contextual appropriateness:** Is it suitable for the audience, domain, circumstances, and stakes?
3. **Safety and content boundaries:** Does it avoid facilitating harm, exploitation, coercion, deception, abuse, or other inappropriate content?
4. **Evidence integrity:** Are factual claims, software claims, and provenance represented honestly?

A failed assessment blocks the output. An unknown or unavailable assessment requires review; it does not silently pass. The deterministic module does not itself understand or moderate natural language. Production use must connect these assessment fields to trusted, tested evaluators and preserve their results.

## Software and operational truth boundary

Daisy must not invent or imply that a file, API, dependency, integration, test, build, deployment, merge, database operation, or security control exists or succeeded. Claims of actual testing, execution, or deployment require corresponding evidence references. A simulation cannot be reported as a real test. A proposed patch is not an applied patch. A successful compile is not proof of production behavior.

## Fail-closed decisions

- **ALLOW:** All required assessments pass and label-specific evidence rules are satisfied.
- **REVIEW_REQUIRED:** No assessment has failed, but at least one required assessment is unknown or unavailable.
- **BLOCKED:** A required assessment fails; task/context is missing; a VERIFIED claim lacks evidence; simulated work is misrepresented; execution is claimed without evidence; or executable work lacks explicit authorization.

## Integration boundary

CICAP is intended to apply before Daisy responses are released and before outputs are passed to software tools, execution pipelines, Discover-Engine, or other SolveX services. Downstream consumers must preserve the decision and evidence label; they must not treat a proposed or simulated result as verified. Human authorization remains required for consequential operations.

## Current implementation limitation

The repository implementation provides typed modes, labels, evidence references, and deterministic ALLOW / REVIEW_REQUIRED / BLOCKED decisions. It does **not** by itself provide semantic task-relevance detection, content moderation, or a complete runtime interception point. Those evaluator and wiring integrations must be implemented and tested before claiming CICAP is enforced across every Daisy surface.
